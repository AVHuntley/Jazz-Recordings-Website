  His style is a bit more careful and relaxed than that of Dizzy Gillespie. His lines are smoother and longer. His style is somewhat more modern sounding and foreshadows later hard bop trumpet styles.

- heading: James Louis "J.J." Johnson
  prose: |-
    He was one of the first trombonists to embrace bebop. He met Fats Novarro while working in a band led by Snookum Russell cir. 1942. Novarro encouraged Johnson to play more like Lester Young (ts).

    Johnson was involved in third stream jazz in later decades. He also scored some movies in the 1970s.

    Johnson wrote an exercises and etudes book, which trombonists may be interested in:
    <https://www.halleonard.com/product/viewproduct.action?itemid=842018>

- heading: Sonny Stitt
  prose: |-
    Very much influenced by Charlie Parker. Difficult to tell them apart on alto. Stitt for a while switched to tenor to avoid the comparison.

    For Miles's *Birth of the Cool* , Stitt was first choice for alto chair, but Gerry Mulligan suggested Lee Konitz instead to retain originality of performance.

    Huge discography. Recorded way more than Charlie Parker (he lived longer too). Check out his discography on Wikipedia:
    <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonny_Stitt>

- heading: Dexter Gordon & Wardell Gray
  artists:
  - name: Dexter Gordon
    born: 1923
    died: 1990
  - name: Wardell Gray
    born: 1921
    died: 1955
  prose: |-
    Dexter Gordon was perhaps the first tenor saxophonist to be recognized as a bebop player. He's often remembered as a West Coast figure; he was born in Los Angeles, CA. His tone is thicker and deeper tone than Lester Young's. His lines are more melodic and predictable than Charlie Parker's. He was featured as an actor in the move *Round Midnight* (1986).

    Wardell Gray was also a Los Angeles figure, though he was originally from Oklahoma City. His "big break" was joining Earl Hines's big band in 1943. He moved to LA cir. 1946, and while there, met and recorded with Charlie Parker. Gray's style is often described as a transitional between swing and bop.

    Gordon and Gray both dealt with a heroin addiction. Gordon even spent some time in prison on drug-related charges. Grey died under somewhat mysterious circumstances: it was probably an overdose, but his body was discovered outside of town and had suffered major injuries.

  prose: |-
    Stanley Gayetzki. From Philadelphia, then New York City.

    Has a light, “fluffy” tone, uncharacteristic of bebop. Heralded the “cool” style.

    Later would make *Getz/Gilberto* , an important bossa nova album.

    Played in Woody Herman's band (as one of the “four brothers”).
