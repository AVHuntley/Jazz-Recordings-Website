  artists:
  - name: Roy Eldridge
    born: 1911
    died: 1989
  prose: |-
    David Roy Eldridge. Born in Pittsburgh, moved to NYC when 19. Was “fiercely competitive in jam sessions, including those at New York's Minton's Playhouse” [(Bierman, p.101)](bibliography.html#bierman-2016)

    Played with Fletcher Henderson for a few months in 1930.

    “His sophisticated use of harmony, including the use of tritone substitutions, his virtuosic solos exhibiting a departure from the smooth and lyrical style of earlier jazz trumpet innovator Louis Armstrong, and his strong impact on Dizzy Gillespie mark him as one of the most influential musicians of the swing era and a precursor of bebop.” (Wikipedia)

    “Roy came along after Louis had already begun to influence every player. Roy took Louis' influence and brought it out of the traditional "New Orleans" realm, heading a movement that would help lead the horn into more modern, evolving styles. He helped to define the instrument for the Big Band and Swing Era of the 1930's and 40's” (
    <https://www.wwbw.com/the-music-room/the-jazz-trumpet-lineage-7-major-players-who-changed-and-defined-the-instrument>
    )

- heading: Lester Young
  artists:
  - name: Lester Young
    born: 1909
    died: 1959
  prose: |-
    Nicknamed “Prez”

    Gained fame in the Count Basie Orchestra. Toured with King Oliver. Frequently collaborated and was friends with Billie Holiday.

    Compared to Hawkins, Young is described as a “horizontal” player – he plays more stepwise, using more seconds. He sounds more melodic, lyrical, mellow. He uses less vibrato than Hawkins. This kind of playing was a major influence on Charlie Parker.

    We previously heard him with Count Basie on “Broadway.”

    Famously wore a [porkpie hat](https://www.google.com/search?q=porkpie+hat&client=firefox-b-1-d&sxsrf=ACYBGNS6BSI46MdMwftKD8pLDT1t5pyzlA:1567955095059&source=lnms&tbm=isch&sa=X&ved=0ahUKEwj9wdvzv8HkAhUI7awKHa7yD0UQ_AUIEigB&biw=1920&bih=944) (which inspired the title of the Mingus song “Goodbye Porkpie Hat”)

    Was influenced by Frankie Trumbauer, especially his solo on “Singin' the Blues,” which we heard previously.

  - name: Charlie Christian

  prose: |-
    Charlie Christian was the first electric guitar player in jazz. He played for some time in Benny Goodman's band.

    His single-note style phrasing was influential on bebop artists. According to Gene Lee: “Many critics and musicians consider that Christian was one of the founding fathers of bebop, or if not that, at least a precursor to it.” He often played at Minton’s Playhouse.
