  - name: Benny Goodman
    born: 1909
    died: 1986
  prose: |-
    Benjamin David Goodman

    Maybe the most popular musician of the swing era, especially among white people.

    Sometimes criticized for being too clean and unadventurous. *Too* good!

    Worked as a session musician and recorded a few albums with a band before getting a gig as one of three bands playing on the radio show *Let’s Dance.* Since he needed new arrangements, he hired Fletcher Henderson to write some and to teach his band how to play them.

    “Goodman's portion of the program from New York, at 12:30 a.m. Eastern Time, aired too late to attract a large East Coast audience. However, unknown to him, the time slot gave him an avid following on the West Coast” (Wikipedia)

    He put on a tour across the country. On August 21, 1935, in Los Angeles at the Palomar Ballroom, he had a three-week engagement that was very well-received. The first set was stock arrangements, the second was “hot” music. Gene Krupa reportedly said, “If we're gonna die, Benny, let's die playing our own thing.” This gig is often cited as marking the beginning of the swing era.

- heading: Count Basie
  artists:
  - name: Count Basie
    born: 1904
    died: 1984
  prose: |-
    William James "Count" Basie was the pianist in Bennie Moten's band. When Moten died, Basie took over.

    This band lasted a long time. In fact, they still tour today as a ghost band. The Basie band was at UNT in January 2019. Here's a video:

    [The Legendary Count Basie Orchestra at UNT pt 1](https://youtu.be/O2XHJSlCnrg)

    This band practically defines the [Kansas City jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_jazz) sound:

    - Four-to-the-floor swing feel
    - Emphasis on soloing
    - "Head" arrangements - frequently performed without a written score and arranged on the spot
    - Emphasis on the blues.
    - Use of riffs - repeating phrases used as part of the "head" or as backgrounds behind the soloist.

  prose: |-
    The “main attraction” to Andy Kirk’s band [(Sultanof, p.57)](bibliography.html#sultanof-2017)

    Arranged for Duke Ellington and Benny Goodman.

    Was a teacher, friend, and mentor to Charlie Parker, Thelonious Monk, Miles Davis, Tadd Dameron, Bud Powell, and Dizzy Gillespie (all of whom we will cover in Unit 2)

    First recording as a leader would be in 1945 with *The Zodiac Suite* .

    Andy Kirk (1898-1992) was a member of Dark Clouds of Joy in Dallas, then formed his own band in Kansas called Clouds of Joy. Played in the Kansas City style.

- heading: Glenn Miller
  artists:
  - name: Glenn Miller
    born: 1904
    died: 1944
  prose: |-
    Trombonist

    He was the best-selling recording artist from 1939 to 1943.

    In just four years Glenn Miller scored 23 number-one hits - more than Elvis Presley (18 No. 1s, 38 top 10s) and the Beatles (20 No. 1s, 33 top 10s) did in their careers

    Died (probably) when his plane went missing over the English Channel in 1944 (during WWII)

    His iconic technique was to have the clarinet play a melodic line with a tenor saxophone holding the same note, with three other saxophones harmonized within a single octave.
