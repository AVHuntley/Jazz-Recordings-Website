  - name: Lennie Tristano
    born: 1919
    died: 1978
  prose: |-
    Progenitor of the cool style and/or innovative bebop player. Not all writers agree about his place in history, but they almost all discuss him.

    He was blind - born with weak eyesight, possibly because his mother had the 1918–19 flu during pregnancy. He contracted measles at 6 years old, which may have worsened his eyesight. By 9 or 10, he was totally blind with glaucoma (optic nerve damage).

    He earned a bachelor’s and started his master’s in music in Chicago. He moved to NYC in 1946.

    Tristano was known for his work as an educator. Had a very disciplined method.

    A lot of people didn’t like him. His music was criticized as too weird, too cold, too abstract. Among those who expressed distaste for his music are Oscar Peterson, Tadd Dameron, Earl Hines, Dave Brubeck, Nat “King” Cole, and even Stan Kenton.

    Tristano was also critical of other musicians. For example, note his comments about Coltrane as remembered by Dave Liebman in the interview linked below.

    Interview with Dave Liebman in which he discusses taking lessons with Tristano:
    <http://davidliebman.com/home/interviews/from-jazz-improv-magazine-2001/>

    In 1951, Tristano moved into a loft in NYC at 317 East 32 nd Street. He converted part of it into a recording studio.

- heading: Modern Jazz Quartet
  prose: |-
    Members were in Gillespie’s Big Band – originally with Ray Brown (bass) and Kenny Clarke (drums)

    Performed for over 40 years together!

    Wore tuxedos; tried to present themselves as “dignified.” Wanted to elevate jazz to the level of “classical” music.

    In 1946, John Lewis (piano), Milt Jackson (vibraphone), Ray Brown (bass), and Kenny Clarke (drums), all members of Dizzy Gillespie's big band, formed a quartet as a side project. By 1951, the combo was recording as the Milt Jackson Quartet. In 1952, Percy Heath replaced Brown on bass, and in late 1952 they changed the name to Modern Jazz Quartet. In 1955, the final switch to the band's lineup occurred as Connie Kay (drums) replaced Clarke. (Wikipedia)

    Final recording was in 1993

    [(Lyons, p.210)](bibliography.html#lyons-1980)
