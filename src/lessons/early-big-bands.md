- heading: Paul Whiteman
  artists:
  - name: Paul Whiteman
    born: 1890
    died: 1967
  prose: |-
    Was publicized as the “King of Jazz”

    Played viola in the Denver and San Francisco Symphony Orchestras. Directed a navy band during WWI. After the war he formed the Paul Whiteman Orchestra.

    Started off in San Francisco. In 1920, moved to New York, where he started making records (for Victor Talking Machine Company). The records made him famous.

    Whiteman’s music is often criticized for being too orchestrated and therefore not “jazz.” Nevertheless, it was the first jazz music thousands of Americans ever heard.

    Wrote more than 3000 arrangements!

    It was Whiteman who commissioned George Gershwin’s *Rhapsody in Blue* , which he premiered.

- heading: Fletcher Henderson
  artists:
  - name: Fletcher Henderson
    born: 1897
    died: 1952
  prose: |-
    Despite extensive early musical training, he pursued chemistry in college and as a first career. Subbing as a pianist occasionally for his New York apartment mate led to a steady gig. Then Henderson realized music was more profitable than chemistry.

    Don Redman was an important arranger – he grouped reeds and brass into separate sections and played them off each other. Dixieland, by contrast, focused on interweaving solo voices. The section became a new building block for jazz arrangements.

    In 1924, Armstrong joined the band. The combination of Armstrong’s solos, Henderson’s band, and Redman’s arrangements made them known for a time as the best African-American band in New York.

    Henderson later arranged for Benny Goodman’s band.

- heading: Duke Ellington
  artists:
  - name: Duke Ellington
    born: 1899
    died: 1974
  prose: |-
    Edward Kennedy "Duke" Ellington

    Considered by many to be America’s greatest composer. (He’s on the 2009 District of Columbia quarter!)

    Played in, then led, a band called the Washingtonians. In 1927, got a gig as the house band at the Cotton Club, a plantation-themed, “whites-only” establishment.

    Active in Harlem, and so considered part of the Harlem Renaissance, though jazz musicians and authors did not regularly mingle. Especially in the 20s, jazz was still lower-class music.

    Music is identifiable for use of effects (growls, wahs), emphasis on “mood music,” “jungle” music, and “exotic” sounds.

    Considered longest-lived big band in jazz history. Personnel rarely changed.

    Composed/arranged for specific band members.
