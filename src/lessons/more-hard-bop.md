  - name: Benny Golson

  prose: |-
    Golson was in Bull Moose Jackson’s combo in 1951 along with Tadd Dameron. Dameron formed his own group in 1953, and Golson joined. Golson joined Blakey’s Jazz Messengers in 1958. Left in 1959, “prodded in part by Blakey’s expressed distaste for the overly arranged drum parts on his charts” [(Gioia, p.289)](bibliography.html#gioia-2011) .

    The Jazztet with Art Farmer and Curtis Fuller existed 1959-1962 and again 1982-late 80s.

    Less funky than the Messengers, more song-oriented, modeled after Tadd Dameron’s sound.

    Golson provided a lot of the arrangements, but Farmer took the largest share of the soloing responsibilities.

- heading: Lee Morgan
  artists:
  - name: Lee Morgan
    born: 1938
    died: 1972
  prose: |-
    Edward Lee Morgan

    Joined Dizzy Gillespie’s big band in 1956 while still a teenager.

    Joined Blakey’s Jazz Messengers 1958-1961.

    Was killed by his partner, Helen Moore, at Slug’s Saloon, a nightclub in NY, between sets. It was very snowy that day, so the ambulance was tragically slow to arrive, and Morgan bled to death.

    Style was “filled with the blues and funky phrases mixed with a bebop sensibility. He played hard with an intense edge and an aggressive and fat sound.” [(Bierman, p.233)](bibliography.html#bierman-2016)

    Barry Harris was not just an important pianist of this era. He was also a dedicated educator. His master classes are among the best jazz videos on the internet. Here's an example:

    [Feeling the "and"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_uMNrujMdJU&t=285s)

- heading: Charles Mingus
  artists:
  - name: Charles Mingus
    born: 1922
    died: 1979
  prose: |-
    Heard already with Dizzy and Bird at Massey Hall date (“Salt Peanuts”), with Lionel Hampton “Mingus Fingers,” and as an example of Avant-Garde jazz with “Don’t Be Afraid, the Clown’s Afraid Too.”

    Has the best song titles: “If Charlie Parker Was a Gunslinger, There’d Be a Whole Lot of Dead Copycats,” “Oh Lord Don’t Let Them Drop That Atomic Bomb On Me,” “The Shoes Of The Fisherman’s Wife Are Some Jive Ass Slippers,” “All The Things You Could Be By Now If Sigmund Freud’s Wife Was Your Mother,” “Don’t Be Afraid, the Clown’s Afraid Too.”

    Wanted to be an orchestral musician but found that to be unattainable for black musicians at the time.

    Known for his use of collective improvisation in eclectic styles. His brilliant writing and emphasis of the strengths of his musicians cause some to compare him to Duke Ellington.

    Today, the Mingus Big Band (Unit 5) continues to play his compositions.
