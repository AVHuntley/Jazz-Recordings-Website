  In this unit, we explore contemporary horn players (beyond saxophonists) and ensembles that have shaped the modern jazz landscape. We’ll also look at larger ensembles that continue evolving jazz orchestration, arrangements, and repertoire.

  artists:
  - name: Wycliffe Gordon
    born: 1967
  prose: |-
    Wycliffe Gordon is a trombonist, composer, bandleader, and educator. [Read his website biography](https://wycliffegordon.com/bio/) for a full list of his accomplishments. He gained prominence with Wynton Marsalis’s band (1989–1995) and was a founding member of the Jazz at Lincoln Center Orchestra. He even arranged NPR’s “All Things Considered” theme.

    Gordon often focuses on early jazz styles, employing “tailgate” trombone techniques and plunger mutes reminiscent of Joe “Tricky Sam” Nanton. He’s heavily influenced by Louis Armstrong, having recorded multiple albums dedicated to Armstrong’s music.

- heading: Dave Douglas
  artists:
  - name: Dave Douglas
    born: 1963
  prose: |-
    Dave Douglas is a trumpeter, composer, and educator. [Read his Grove article](https://libproxy.library.unt.edu:2147/10.1093/gmo/9781561592630.article.A2228230) . He attended Berklee, NEC, and NYU. He played with Horace Silver in the 80s and has earned numerous awards, including multiple Trumpet Player of the Year awards from *DownBeat* and Jazz Journalists Association.

    Douglas runs [Greenleaf Music](https://greenleafmusic.com) , a label and online hub for jazz, releasing music by Linda Oh, Donny McCaslin, Kneebody, and others. He also hosts the “A Noise from the Deep” podcast.

- heading: Terence Blanchard
  artists:
  - name: Terence Blanchard
    born: 1962
  prose: |-
    Terence Blanchard, from New Orleans, studied at Rutgers and took Wynton Marsalis’s place in the Jazz Messengers in 1982. He’s a prolific film composer (notably for Spike Lee) and contributed to Disney’s *The Princess and the Frog* . With multiple Grammys and nominations, Blanchard bridges jazz, film music, and activism.

    His 2015 album *Breathless* (with the E-Collective) was dedicated to Eric Garner. The 2018 follow-up, *Live* , addressed gun violence. Check out the documentary-style video (segments at 0–4:38, 10:52–..., 23:48–...).

- heading: SFJazz Collective
  prose: |-
    The SFJazz Collective is the resident ensemble of SFJazz, formed in 2004 by Joshua Redman. They focus annually on the music of a jazz great. Their rosters have included top-tier musicians like Miguel Zenón, Avishai Cohen, Mark Turner, Stefon Harris, and many others. Each year, they also commission new works from the members.

    In 2011, they recorded an album featuring the music of Stevie Wonder alongside new compositions.
