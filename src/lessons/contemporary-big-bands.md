intro: |-
  In the current decade, the big band remains a comparatively minor part of the jazz world. But that doesn't mean it has gone extinct. Though only a few big bands continue to tour the way they did in the 1930s and 40s, many communities maintain **rehearsal bands** . Several prominent players and arrangers still occasionally record big band albums. And some bands even continue the legacy of their deceased figurehead in the form of a **ghost band** .

  The [Grammy Award for Best Large Jazz Ensemble Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Large_Jazz_Ensemble_Album) is awarded every year. The list of nominees for and recipients of this award is one way I determined what bands to feature in this lesson and [the next]($WIKI_REFERENCE$/pages/5-dot-2-contemporary-big-bands-pt-2 "5.2 - Contemporary Big Bands Pt. 2") .

  ## Vocabulary
  **Rehearsal band**
  - a big band that meets regularly, often weekly at the same location, to play for fun and practice, if not always profit. They rarely tour, though many sometimes record. Their personnel is not always consistent.

  **Ghost band**
  - a band that plays under the name of a deceased leader. Several bands from the swing era exist in this form, including the [Glenn Miller Orchestra](https://glennmillerorchestra.com/) , the [Count Basie Orchestra](https://www.thecountbasieorchestra.com/) , the [Ellington Orchestra](http://www.dukeellington.com/orchestra.html) , and the [Mingus Big Band](https://www.charlesmingus.com/mingus-big-band) .

- heading: Mingus Big Band
  prose: |-
    The Mingus Big Band is one of a few currently active ensembles specializing in the music of Charles Mingus. The other two bands are the [Mingus Orchestra](https://www.charlesmingus.com/orchestra) and the [Mingus Dynasty](https://www.charlesmingus.com/dynasty) . The three bands perform on [Mondays at the Jazz Standard](https://www.jazzstandard.com/programs/#Mingus) , a venue in NYC. Each band was formed and organized by Sue Mingus, Charles's widow. She put together the Mingus Big Band in 1997.

    The popularity of the Mingus Big Band was cemented when their 2010 album *Live at Jazz Standard* won the Grammy for Best Large Jazz Ensemble album.

    Its ever-evolving personnel list has included such greats as Randy Brecker, Donny McCaslin, Gary Smulyan, Orrin Evans, Kenny Drew, Jr., and Jeff "Tain" Watts.

  prose: |-
    Gordon Goodwin is a Los Angeles-based pianist, saxophonist, composer and arranger who has worked extensively in the film and television industry. He attended Cal State Northridge (CSUN) and produced music for several blockbuster films, including *The Incredibles* . He formed the Big Phat Band in 1999, mostly, as he writes on [his website](http://www.bigphatband.com/about-bpbbio) , "with the intention of recording a record and nothing more. I wanted to document the music I was writing for the big band genre, but no way did I want to hassle with doing live gigs..." A live performance at his alma mater CSUN convinced him to keep the band together.

    In 2015, the Big Phat Band won the Grammy for Best Large Jazz Ensemble Album with *Life in the Bubble* . Prior to that, the band's second album, *XXL* , was nominated for the award, and the composition "Hunting Wabbits" was nominated for Best Instrumental Composition.

- heading: Arturo O’Farrill & the Afro Latin Jazz Orchestra
  artists:
  - name: Arturo O’Farrill
    born: 1960
  prose: |-
    Arturo O'Farrill is a pianist, composer, and educator who directs the Afro Latin Jazz Alliance, formerly the Afro Latin Jazz Orchestra. Read a [short biography on his website](http://www.arturoofarrill.com/about-arturo) . He is the son of [Chico O’Farrill](https://libproxy.library.unt.edu:2147/10.1093/gmo/9781561592630.article.A2242503) (1921-2001), a renowned Cuban musician who composed for Machito and arranged for Dizzy Gillespie and Stan Kenton.

    Chico and Arturo formed the Afro-Cuban Jazz Orchestra in the 1990s. In 2002, this band became a resident orchestra at Jazz at Lincoln Center. In 2007, they split from Jazz at Lincoln Center and formed under a new organization, the Afro Latin Jazz Alliance. As their website explains:

    In early 2007, O’Farrill and the Afro Latin Jazz Orchestra (ALJO) left Jazz at Lincoln Center to pursue the twin goals of developing new audiences for big band Latin jazz and creating a robust educational program for young performers. With the support of prominent leaders from the worlds of jazz and Latin culture, O’Farrill launched ALJA to serve as a non-profit organization that could advance both the performance and educational aspects of this uniquely Pan-American art form.

    Their album *Cuba: The Conversation Continues* is dedicated to the complicated relationship between Cuba and the United States. It was nominated for the 2016 Grammy for Best Large Jazz Ensemble album. Watch this video from *Jazz Night in America* about the project (watch at least the first five minutes):
