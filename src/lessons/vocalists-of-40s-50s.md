- heading: Sarah Vaughan
  artists:
  - name: Sarah Vaughan
    born: 1924
    died: 1990
  prose: |-
    She had a couple nicknames: “The Divine One,” and “Sassy.”

    Her style reflects less focus on coherent interpretations of a song’s lyrics and more on the manipulation of her voice for musical reasons. It helps that she was such a virtuosic singer. She is often described as operatic; she had a four-octave range, an open tone, and used **terminal vibrato** .

    She was from New Jersey. She won the Apollo talent contest in 1942 (the same Apollo contest that kickstarted Ella Fitzgerald's career). This is how she got noticed by Earl Hines, who hired her for his band, which included Charlie Parker, Dizzy Gillespie, and Billy Eckstine. Vaughan left Hines's band in 1944 to pursue a solo career.

- heading: Betty Carter
  artists:
  - name: Betty Carter
    born: 1929
    died: 1998
  prose: |-
    Betty Carter grew up in Detroit. She was in Lionel Hampton's band, and she performed with Dizzy Gillespie and Charlie Parker.

    She was given the name Lillie Mae Jones. Her stage name for many years was Lorraine Carter. While in Lionel Hampton's band, Hampton's wife gave her the nickname “Betty Bebop.” Combining those two stage names created the name we know her as: Betty Carter.

    Wonderful articulation. Audible consonants, wide open vowels.

  prose: |-
    Carmen McRae was in Benny Carter’s band. She was an intermission pianist and singer at Minton’s. In 1939, McRae also won the Apollo talent contest.

    She employed behind-the-beat phrasing and sings in a way that can make lyrics sound ironic.

    Wide vibrato at ends of phrases.

- heading: Mel Tormé
  artists:
  - name: Mel Tormé
    born: 1925
    died: 1999
  prose: |-
    Mel Tormé is sometimes called “The Velvet Fog" for his smooth voice and lyrical phrasing.

    He was inspired by Frank Sinatra. Many consider his style to be part of the West Coast Cool camp.

    He sometimes takes long improvised solos, and he's not afraid to use his falsetto range.

    You probably know at least one original composition of his: he wrote “The Christmas Song” (chestnuts roasting on an open fire...).
