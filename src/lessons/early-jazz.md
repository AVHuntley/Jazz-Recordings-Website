intro: |-
  This music covered in this section exemplifies some of the earliest that is widely recognized as jazz. This is the common foundation of all the other (sub)genres this course covers.

  Here's some useful vocabulary:

  [Three-horn front line](vocabulary.html#three-horn-front-line): Trumpet/cornet, clarinet, and trombone.  Typically the trumpet plays the melody, the clarinet plays decorative  lines above the melody, and the trombone plays close to the root of the chord, below the melody. Compare this instrumentation to that of the contemporary big band.  
  term: Rhythm section
  definition: Tuba/Bass; Banjo/Guitar/Piano. Drums were only sometimes recorded. It was hard to record drum set with early technology, so percussionists often played on wood blocks.  
  term: “Tailgate” trombone
  definition: a style of playing that included many trombone specific techniques such as growling, scoops, falls, and slides. Named for the way Dixieland trombonists would often sit and play at the back of an open trailer.  
  term: Stop time
  definition: the band plays a series of coordinated hits behind the soloist, often on the first beat of the measure, and the soloist continues, temporarily without accompaniment.  
  term: Solo break
  definition: the rhythm section stops playing behind a soloist for a brief period, often about half-way through a song form.

- heading: Louis Armstrong
  artists:
  - name: Louis Armstrong
    born: 1901
    died: 1971
  prose: |-
    Born in New Orleans. Came from a difficult background. Eventually found himself running errands for his idol, King Oliver. Got some lessons in return.

    In 1917, when Oliver moved to Chicago, Armstrong took over for Oliver in Kid Ory's band. Eventually was hired by Oliver as 2 nd cornet and moved to Chicago (where he influenced Bix Beiderbeck).

    In 1924, moved to New York to join Fletcher Henderson's band.

    In 1925, returned to Chicago. Between 1925 and 1928, recorded the Hot Five and Hot Seven sessions.

    Here, he met and became close with pianist Earl “Fatha” Hines (1903-1983).

    Armstrong is often called the first great jazz soloist. He was rhythmically innovative, a natural syncopator. He ornamented and embellished melodies.

    **Scat** singing is vocal improvisation with wordless vocables, meaningless syllables, or without words at all.

  - name: Bix Beiderbecke
    born: 1903
    died: 1931
  prose: |-
    Most well-known Chicago-era musicians. Incidentally, neither was from Chicago!

    Beiderbecke was from Iowa. He was sent to a private academy in Chicago to curb his jazz habit. It had the opposite effect. He learned by ear, and only began to read music later, when he had to play in bands.

    Compared to Armstrong, Beiderbecke has a more “lyrical sound that projected a sense of 'subdued passion'” [(Lawn, p.105)](bibliography.html#lawn-2013) . Uses fewer growls and less vibrato.

    Trumbauer also sounded “smooth, lyrical and cool, and his rhythmic phrasing rarely demonstrated much syncopation or think, wide vibrato.” [(Lawn, p.107)](bibliography.html#lawn-2013)

    Characteristics of the **Chicago style** (compared to New Orleans):

    - Refined sound and instrumental polish
    - Greater emphasis on arrangement and well-rehearsed ensemble performance.
    - Increased emphasis on individual soloists
    - New Orleans was “hot”; Chicago was “cool.”
