- heading: Ornette Coleman
  artists:
  - name: Ornette Coleman
    born: 1930
    died: 2015
  prose: |-
    Called his approach **“harmolodic”** – harmony + movement + melody. As Coleman described it in the liner notes of *Dancing in Your Head* , "This means the rhythms, harmonics and tempos are all equal in relationship and independent melodies at the same time."

    [Here's a blog post that addresses the concept of "harmolodics."](http://harderbop.blogspot.com/2012/10/defining-harmolodics.html)

    One of the great innovators in jazz.

    Promoted by MJQ members John Lewis and Percy Heath.

    Grew up in Fort Worth, TX.

    Don Cherry plays an odd instrument called a pocket cornet (he called it a "pocket trumpet"). It's like a trumpet but, as its name implies, much smaller.

    Gig at the Five Spot in 1959 one month after release of *The Shape of Jazz to Come* . Here, Mingus and Miles saw him play (as did many others). Scheduled to last two weeks. Got extended to two-and-a-half months.

    Mingus and Davis were critical. “It’s like not having anything to do with what’s around you, and being right in your own world” - Mingus

  - title: Lonely Woman
    performer: Ornette Coleman
    composer: Ornette Coleman
    recorded: May 22, 1959
    album: The Shape of Jazz to Come
    personnel:
    - [Ornette Coleman, alto saxophone]
    - [Don Cherry, cornet]
    - [Charlie Haden, bass]
    - [Billy Higgins, drums]
    spotify: 23aDP04QX8MAHA99FLaPtN
    intro: |-
      No chordal instrument. A jazz standard (one of the oddest?). Ornette Coleman is considered a founding figure of the "free jazz" style, and this album, *The Shape of Jazz to Come* , is one of the most influential albums of the style.

      - Things "Lonely Woman" has in common with traditional jazz:
        - It begins and ends with the same melody, which has the form AABA.
        - The form is head, solos, head.
        - The drummer (Higgins) plays a familiar swing pattern.
        - The instrumentation is typical for a jazz quartet, with the notable absence of a chordal instrument (e.g., piano or guitar).
      - Things “Lonely Woman” does differently:
        - The number of measures in each section is not predetermined; the musicians stretch or compress the length of each section as they wish.
        - There is no clearly defined harmony - no specified chords.
        - The bass player does not play a walking pattern.
    guide:
    - ['0:00', 'Intro, Higgins (drums) and Haden (bass) begin the song together. At first the drums play only sparsely. About 7 seconds in, Higgins begins to play a more rapid swing pattern. Haden plays two notes at a time on the bass, a technique called a **double-stop** .']
    - ['0:19', 'Head In, First A - Coleman and Cherry play the melody together in octaves. The melody seems to float above the accompaniment, not quite in time, but not quite out-of-time either.']
    - ['0:44', 'Head In, Second A']
    - ['1:09', 'Head In, B - Coleman plays the B section of the head. Cherry and Haden play rising chromatic notes that build tension and create an ominous mood.']
    - ['1:22', 'Head In, final A - Coleman and Cherry again play the A section together, in octaves.']
    - ['1:47', 'Solo, A - Coleman takes a solo over the A section of the song. There is no set number of measures per section, so this section is of an indeterminate length. We can tell that he''s soloing over the A section because Haden is playing the same notes on the bass that he played during the A sections of the head.']
    - ['2:21', 'Solo, B - The beginning of the B section is heralded by the rising chromatic notes played by Cherry and Haden.']

- heading: Cecil Taylor
  artists:
  - name: Cecil Taylor
    born: 1929
    died: 2018
  prose: |-
    Background in classical music.

    Had gig at the Five Spot in 1956 for 6 weeks.

    Incredible virtuosity. Known for his flurries of notes and his freely improvised solo concerts.

    Has recorded at least a couple drum + piano duets. One with Max Roach, another with Tony Williams.

    Known (in part) for his entirely “free” improvisations on solo piano during concerts which would often last 3+ hours.

    Thought about music in “Unit Structures.”

- heading: Sun Ra
  artists:
  - name: Sun Ra
    born: 1914
    died: 1993
  prose: |-
    Born: Herman Poole Blount.

    Legal name: Le Sony’r Ra.

    From Alabama. He was a prodigious pianist and musician as a child.

    He claimed he was an alien from Saturn. He had a “vision” when in college in which he was abducted by aliens that told him to stop attending college and to play music.

    “Ra” comes from the Egyptian god of the sun. Sun Ra was obsessed with Egyptian occultism. Considered part of an aesthetic/philosophy called Afrofuturism, which may also include Deltron 3030 and the superhero Black Panther.

    The Arkestra continues to perform without Sun Ra to this day.
