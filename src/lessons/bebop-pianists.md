- heading: Thelonious Monk
  artists:
  - name: Thelonious Monk
    born: 1917
    died: 1982
  prose: |-
    Monk grew up in Manhattan's West Side. He was largely self-taught, though he was mentored by Mary Lou Williams (remember that lick in "Walkin' and Swingin' that Monk later turned into "Rhythm-a-ning"?).

    Monk was the house pianist at Minton's, with Kenny Clarke (d) Nick Fenton (b) and Joe Guy (t).

    Compared to Powell, Monk played spare, “choppy” lines. Perhaps more a descendant of Ellington's piano style (remember Ellington's solo on Ko-Ko?). Listen for clusters (minor seconds), whole tone scale runs, scale degree #4 (or b5), and sparse - often single note - comping.

    “Monk has a beat like the ocean waves – no matter how sudden, spasmodic, or obscure his little inventions, he rocks irresistibly on.” – Pal Bacon for *Record Changer* [(Lyons, p.189)](bibliography.html#lyons-1980)

    Monk languished in obscurity for many years, partially as a result of losing his [cabaret card](https://jazztimes.com/features/columns/the-cabaret-card-and-jazz/) in 1941, '51, and '58.  A six-month gig at the Five Spot Café in 1958 helped propel him back into the limelight.

- heading: Bud Powell
  artists:
  - name: Bud Powell
    born: 1924
    died: 1966
  prose: |-
    Earl Rudolph "Bud" Powell

    Raised in Harlem, NYC. Brother was Richie Powell, a pianist who played with (and died with) Clifford Brown.

    It was Monk who introduced Powell to the "emerging circle of bebop musicians forming at Minton's Playhouse," and "Monk treated Powell as his protégé." [(White, p.13)](bibliography.html#white-2016) .

    “If Earl Hines played 'trumpet-style' piano… Powell's right hand lines suggested the alto sax, seemingly a magical transfer of Charlie Parker's searing, tortuous melodic concepts onto the keyboard” [(Lyons, p.195)](bibliography.html#lyons-1980)

- heading: Erroll Garner
  artists:
  - name: Erroll Garner
    born: 1923
    died: 1977
  prose: |-
    Born in Pittsburgh, PA. He began playing the piano at three years old.

    He was largely self-taught, and played by ear. He couldn't read music well.

    Virtuoso with incredible L/R hand coordination. His stride style may be closer to that of swing era musicians than to bebop players.

    His most famous original composition is the standard “Misty.”

    Garner went to the same high school as Billy Strayhorn and Ahmad Jamal.

- heading: Marian McPartland
  artists:
  - name: Marian McPartland
    born: 1918
    died: 2013
  prose: |-
    Born in England. She preferred to learn by ear; she learned to read late.

    She was the host of [*Marian McPartland's* *Piano Jazz*](https://www.npr.org/series/15773266/marian-mcpartland-s-piano-jazz/archive) on NPR from 1978-2011. This is a treasure trove of interviews and duets with important jazz musicians.

    She served in the USO during WWII, following Allies into Europe after D-Day.

    In 2000 she was named a [National Endowment for the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Endowment_for_the_Arts "National Endowment for the Arts") Jazz Master.
