intro: |-
  In this unit, we explore several prominent contemporary saxophonists. These musicians emerged in the late 20th or early 21st century, playing in contexts as diverse as the last Miles Davis band, contemporary funk and fusion ensembles, acoustic post-bop settings, and even collaborations with pop and rock artists like David Bowie. Their careers and styles exemplify the breadth and depth of today’s jazz saxophone tradition.

- heading: Kenny Garrett
  artists:
  - name: Kenny Garrett
    born: 1960
  prose: |-
    Read the [Grove Music Online article about Kenny Garrett](https://libproxy.library.unt.edu:2147/10.1093/gmo/9781561592630.article.A2228299) . Garrett, an alto saxophonist and flutist, started young, playing in the Duke Ellington ghost band as a teenager. He worked with the Jazz Messengers and joined Miles Davis’s final band (1986-1991). Miles praised Garrett as developing a unique style rather than just copying past influences.

    Garrett didn't study formally at a university, yet he received an honorary doctorate from Berklee in 2011. His improvisations feature highly scalar lines, wide range usage, and a blend of funky, bluesy edges with Coltrane- and Tyner-inspired harmony.

- heading: Joshua Redman
  artists:
  - name: Joshua Redman
    born: 1969
  prose: |-
    Read the Grove Music Online article about Joshua Redman. Son of Dewey Redman, Joshua graduated from Harvard in social studies and won the 1991 Thelonious Monk Competition. He declined Yale Law School to pursue music. He led popular quartets and founded the SFJAZZ Collective. Redman’s style adapts to different settings, from complex rhythmic unisons to soft, melodious phrasing.

    Check out his 1994 album *MoodSwing* (with Mehldau, McBride, Blade) for an example of his accessible yet sophisticated style. Later, he explored jazz-funk with the Elastic Band.

- heading: Chris Potter
  artists:
  - name: Chris Potter
    born: 1971
  prose: |-
    Read the [JazzTimes article](https://jazztimes.com/features/profiles/chris-potter-master-of-paradox/) about Chris Potter. A child prodigy, Potter attended the New School and Manhattan School of Music. He frequently tops the *DownBeat* polls for Best Tenor Saxophonist. He placed 3rd in the same Monk competition that Joshua Redman won.

    Known for remarkable technique and creativity, Potter recorded an 11-minute unaccompanied version of "All the Things You Are" that has become a cult favorite. He has worked with Maria Schneider, receiving Grammy nominations for his solos in her orchestra. A collaboration with David Bowie on *Blackstar* introduced him to a wider audience.

- heading: Melissa Aldana
  artists:
  - name: Melissa Aldana
    born: 1988
  prose: |-
    Melissa Aldana, from Santiago, Chile, won the 2013 Thelonious Monk saxophone competition at 24, the first woman and first South American to do so in a non-vocal category. She studied at Berklee after growing up learning saxophone from her father. She performed at UNT in 2018.

    Her 2019 album *Visions* was inspired by Frida Kahlo and includes mostly originals except one standard, “Never Let Me Go.”
